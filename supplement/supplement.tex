\section{Executable interfaces and conventions}
The package \code{memorylab} provides candidate/source records, a catalog, memory profiles, model dispatch, quantum channels, deterministic $T_2$ assessment and network export. Source identities are content hashes with source metadata. The evaluated source is version 0.6.0; its archive identity is recorded in Section S8.

The implemented family labels are atomic ensemble, trapped atom/ion, rare-earth solid, defect spin, molecular spin and negative ion. Their availability is not six independent experimental validations. The negative-ion branch requires an environment-overlap parameter; it does not deduce coherence from neutralization. Temperature ramps, state-dependent acceptance and a complete structure-to-spin--phonon-to-optical-instrument chain are outside the evaluated contract.

Channel compression retains Choi eigenvalues above $10^{-14}$ and rejects eigenvalues below $-10^{-9}$. Default CPTP checks use $10^{-9}$ tolerance; the QuTiP Markov comparison permits $2\times10^{-8}$. State-validity checks use $10^{-9}$. Recorded maximum discrepancies, rather than merely boolean checks, are retained. Numerical agreement is verification of the specified equations, not evidence that omitted experimental physics is negligible.

\section{Mean fidelity does not uniquely determine a channel}
This is a constructive mathematical example, not a second fit to all released experimental states. For the same six-state mean $F_0$, define $p=2(1-F_0)$ and $q=3p/4$. Depolarizing Kraus operators are
\[
K_0=\sqrt{1-3p/4}\,I,\quad
K_x=\sqrt{p/4}\,X,\quad K_y=\sqrt{p/4}\,Y,\quad K_z=\sqrt{p/4}\,Z.
\]
Phase-flip operators are $M_0=\sqrt{1-q}I$ and $M_1=\sqrt qZ$. The latter leaves the two Z-axis states unchanged and gives fidelity $1-q$ for each of the four transverse states. Its six-state mean is $1-2q/3=1-p/2=F_0$.

For identical independent maps on the two halves of a Bell pair,
\begin{align*}
F_B^{\rm dep}&=1-\tfrac32p+\tfrac34p^2,\\
F_B^{\rm phase}&=(1-q)^2+q^2=1-\tfrac32p+\tfrac98p^2,\\
F_B^{\rm phase}-F_B^{\rm dep}&=\tfrac38p^2.
\end{align*}
At $F_0=0.9671968088636844$, $p=0.06560638227263116$ and $q=0.04920478670447337$. Direct Kraus propagation gives $F_B^{\rm dep}=0.9048185746372300$ and $F_B^{\rm phase}=0.9064326486603184$, a difference of 0.0016140740230883. The matrices and values are regenerated by \code{scripts/make\_figures.py}. More extensive tomography and a conditioning model are needed to discriminate experimentally compatible channels.

\section{PR2026 curve diagnostics and negative outcomes}
Four released classical-efficiency curves have 15, 70, 70 and 70 observations for no RF, XX, XY4 and XY16. Efficiencies and their supplied SEM quantities are converted consistently; reported total delays are converted from microseconds to seconds. The fit factor is explicitly one, defining an apparent retrieval-decay scale. No result is converted to homogeneous spin $T_2$.

The Gaussian model is $b+A\exp[-(t/\tau)^\beta]$, with exponential $\beta=1$ or stretched $0.25\leq\beta\leq4$. The implementation uses three starts and bounded log-$\tau$. Every fourth sorted unique time group, using zero-based group indices congruent to three modulo four, is withheld. Thirty parametric bootstrap draws are requested per full-data fit. Status includes the full-data approximate Gaussian goodness diagnostic, holdout residuals, boundaries, observed-time range, reviewed observable mapping and bootstrap stability.

All six RF fits are model mismatches. The no-RF exponential scale lies outside the observed window; the no-RF stretched bootstrap yields only 28 of 30 accepted draws and is inconclusive under the implemented requirement. A zero stored $p$ is numerical underflow, not an exact zero probability. Tiny Gaussian probabilities are conditional on supplied uncertainties and independence; unmodeled correlation/systematics limit the physical interpretation. The source experiment already reports nonexponential behavior. Thus the retained negative results diagnose the tested envelopes, rather than demonstrating that the experimental memory itself fails.

The separate heralded-photon arrays are not qubit process tomography. The source value \code{st}=100 $\mu$s and the article's 100.9 $\mu$s value are retained as an unresolved clock mapping. The paper's nominal spin coherence quoted from other work is not a new lifetime measured from these arrays. Neither $g^{(2)}$ nor extended classical storage is substituted for time-resolved qubit fidelity.

\section{Deterministic and synthetic verification}
The Markov case supplies $\Gamma_1=10$ and $\Gamma_\phi=20$ s$^{-1}$ at hypothetical 4.2 K conditions, giving 40 ms. Rate-box corners use $\Gamma_1\in[8,12]$ and $\Gamma_\phi\in[18,22]$ s$^{-1}$. Their range [35.714286,45.454545] ms is deterministic parameter uncertainty; its $-10.7143\%/+13.6364\%$ deviations are not prediction-error margins.

The finite-bath case has one $^{13}$C spin $I=1/2$, logical projections $m=\pm1/2$, laboratory Zeeman $\omega_z=214163.9829$ Hz and hyperfine $A_{zz}=4000$ Hz at 4.2 K. Other couplings vanish. In the unpolarized limit, the longitudinal model gives $L(t)=\cos(\pi A_{zz}t)$; the finite-temperature implementation uses its exact Gibbs state. Each of free evolution and ideal one-pulse Hahn control evaluates 101 times over 0--1 ms. Free evolution has a first sampled $1/e$ crossing bracket [90,100] $\mu$s and revivals. Hahn control swaps the commuting branches and has no sampled crossing over this finite window. These parameters are specified controls, not inferred properties of a molecular candidate.

The synthetic likelihood control uses $A=0.8$, $b=0$, $\tau=1$ ms, exponential factor one and four known phases $0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2$. Ages are zero plus 20 geometric points from 10 $\mu$s to 4 ms. At every age and phase, 4000 binomial trials are generated with seed 20261010: 84 count observations and 336,000 Bernoulli trials. Group holdout retains all four phases at each withheld age together. Five ages (20 observations) are held out; 64 observations calibrate the holdout fit. The reported full-data fit uses all 84 counts. Sixty requested bootstrap draws all succeed. Recovery is 0.9796631569 ms with conditional 95\% interval [0.9502149602,1.0077371898] ms, held-out Pearson RMSE 0.8632951 and approximate goodness $p=0.9528551$. One recovered generator instance is not a coverage study.

\section{Network controls and work counts}
The Teller integration control uses the actual SeQUeNCe 1.2.0 event kernel and density manager. Each attempt loads an ideal Bell state, schedules retrieval at 18 $\mu$s and applies the calibrated map once. Two nodes have nominal acceptance 0.014112, independent losses and ideal link/herald probability one. Four seeds 20261010--20261013 each have 100,000 attempts at a 1 ms interval. Counts 20, 32, 24 and 11 sum to 87. The fixed-loss expectation is 79.6594176 pairs. The pooled Wilson interval is [0.000176350196,0.000268249181]. Conditional Bell fidelity is 0.9048185746, and the maximum joint-density discrepancy is $1.11\times10^{-16}$. The simulated attempt clock, totaling 400 s, is not wall-clock runtime or a physical network benchmark.

The separate hypothetical profile has write/read acceptance 0.8/0.7, depolarization parameters 0.01/0.02, write/read durations 100/200 $\mu$s and constant 4.2 K stages. It exports ages 0, 100 and 500 $\mu$s; retrieval uses 500 $\mu$s, ideal link/herald factor 0.5, 2000 attempts and 1 ms interval. It produces 321 pairs and Bell fidelity 0.9559176317. Its maximum channel Choi discrepancy is $4.00\times10^{-15}$ and joint-density discrepancy $5.55\times10^{-17}$. These supplied parameters have no experimental calibration in this study.

\section{Reproduction and claim traceability}
The source archive used for this study has SHA-256
\begin{center}\small\texttt{c64b94a573def9d999b65be7201947e9c}\\\texttt{2ce4e496aaf201650d7c1e3d44733aa}.\end{center}
There is no public Git commit or software DOI for this study. A local archive hash is not presented as a public publication identifier.
The repository root contains \code{paper.tex}, \code{supplement/supplement.tex}, \code{figures/}, \code{tables/}, \code{evidence/}, \code{scripts/} and evaluated source in \code{lab/}. \code{REPRODUCIBILITY.md} supplies dependency and LaTeX setup. From the root, a Python environment with the stated packages executes \code{python scripts/reproduce.py}. The script invokes, from \code{lab/}, the experimental baseline command, the offline deterministic command with SeQUeNCe enabled, and all unit tests, then refreshes evidence, figures and tables and checks the headline invariants. An online variant explicitly refreshes remote data and may change metadata bytes; the archived offline path reproduces the pinned study.

\code{scripts/verify\_results.py} checks the reanalysis values, count controls, model statuses and simulated counts, and emits a claim-to-result JSON map. These checks protect the reporting pipeline; they are not extra experimental measurements. Every headline claim links to an executed result or log. Detailed fit outputs retain status, seed, bootstrap success, conditions and limitations. Files predating this article remain under \code{lab/} as historical source outputs and are not all fresh experimental validations. The \code{evidence/} directory is the article's selected result snapshot.

The complete manuscript can be built using \code{make pdf}. Numerical reproduction does not require LaTeX; PDF compilation does not require running the simulator. Figures require Matplotlib. No ML libraries are needed. The software test logs record Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, QuTiP 5.3.1, PySCF 2.14.0, PyCCE 1.1.1 and SeQUeNCe 1.2.0. Optional-adapter test execution does not replace application-specific physical validation.

Future candidate evaluation should retain these distinctions: independently review units, definitions and conditions; anchor Hamiltonian and noise parameters; establish numerical convergence; reproduce time-resolved optical fidelity, success and noise; and assess any proposed improvement under matched operating conditions. The present study supplies the workflow and controls for such work without claiming those future outcomes.
