\section{Executable interfaces and conventions}
The package \code{memorylab} provides candidate/source records, a catalog, memory profiles, model dispatch, quantum channels, deterministic $T_2$ assessment and network export. Source identities are content hashes with source metadata. The base evaluated source is version 0.6.0; its archive identity is recorded in the reproduction section. The additional system evaluation package is version 0.1.0 and retains a separate source fingerprint.

The implemented family labels are atomic ensemble, trapped atom/ion, rare-earth solid, defect spin, molecular spin and negative ion. Their availability is not six independent experimental validations. The negative-ion branch requires an environment-overlap parameter; it does not deduce coherence from neutralization. Temperature ramps, state-dependent acceptance and a complete structure-to-spin--phonon-to-optical-instrument chain are outside the evaluated contract.

Channel compression retains Choi eigenvalues above $10^{-14}$ and rejects eigenvalues below $-10^{-9}$. Default CPTP checks use $10^{-9}$ tolerance; the QuTiP Markov comparison permits $2\times10^{-8}$. State-validity checks use $10^{-9}$. Recorded maximum discrepancies, rather than merely boolean checks, are retained. Numerical agreement is verification of the specified equations, not evidence that omitted experimental physics is negligible.

\section{Mean fidelity does not uniquely determine a channel}
This is a constructive mathematical example, not a second fit to all released experimental states. For the same six-state mean $F_0$, define $p=2(1-F_0)$ and $q=3p/4$. Depolarizing Kraus operators are
\[
K_0=\sqrt{1-3p/4}\,I,\quad
K_x=\sqrt{p/4}\,X,\quad K_y=\sqrt{p/4}\,Y,\quad K_z=\sqrt{p/4}\,Z.
\]
Phase-flip operators are $M_0=\sqrt{1-q}I$ and $M_1=\sqrt qZ$. The latter leaves the two Z-axis states unchanged and gives fidelity $1-q$ for each of the four transverse states. Its six-state mean is $1-2q/3=1-p/2=F_0$.

For identical independent maps on the two halves of a Bell pair,
\begin{align*}
F_B^{\rm dep}&=1-\tfrac32p+\tfrac34p^2,\\
F_B^{\rm phase}&=(1-q)^2+q^2=1-\tfrac32p+\tfrac98p^2,\\
F_B^{\rm phase}-F_B^{\rm dep}&=\tfrac38p^2.
\end{align*}
At $F_0=0.9671968088636844$, $p=0.06560638227263116$ and $q=0.04920478670447337$. Direct Kraus propagation gives $F_B^{\rm dep}=0.9048185746372300$ and $F_B^{\rm phase}=0.9064326486603184$, a difference of 0.0016140740230883. The matrices and values are regenerated by \code{scripts/make\_figures.py}. More extensive tomography and a conditioning model are needed to discriminate experimentally compatible channels.

\section{Deterministic and synthetic verification}
The Markov case supplies $\Gamma_1=10$ and $\Gamma_\phi=20$ s$^{-1}$ at hypothetical 4.2 K conditions, giving 40 ms. Rate-box corners use $\Gamma_1\in[8,12]$ and $\Gamma_\phi\in[18,22]$ s$^{-1}$. Their range [35.714286,45.454545] ms is deterministic parameter uncertainty; its $-10.7143\%/+13.6364\%$ deviations are not prediction-error margins.

The finite-bath case has one $^{13}$C spin $I=1/2$, logical projections $m=\pm1/2$, laboratory Zeeman $\omega_z=214163.9829$ Hz and hyperfine $A_{zz}=4000$ Hz at 4.2 K. Other couplings vanish. In the unpolarized limit, the longitudinal model gives $L(t)=\cos(\pi A_{zz}t)$; the finite-temperature implementation uses its exact Gibbs state. Each of free evolution and ideal one-pulse Hahn control evaluates 101 times over 0--1 ms. Free evolution has a first sampled $1/e$ crossing bracket [90,100] $\mu$s and revivals. Hahn control swaps the commuting branches and has no sampled crossing over this finite window. These parameters are specified controls, not inferred properties of a molecular candidate.

The synthetic likelihood control uses $A=0.8$, $b=0$, $\tau=1$ ms, exponential factor one and four known phases $0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2$. Ages are zero plus 20 geometric points from 10 $\mu$s to 4 ms. At every age and phase, 4000 binomial trials are generated with seed 20261010: 84 count observations and 336,000 Bernoulli trials. Group holdout retains all four phases at each withheld age together. Five ages (20 observations) are held out; 64 observations calibrate the holdout fit. The reported full-data fit uses all 84 counts. Sixty requested bootstrap draws all succeed. Recovery is 0.9796631569 ms with conditional 95\% interval [0.9502149602,1.0077371898] ms, held-out Pearson RMSE 0.8632951 and approximate goodness $p=0.9528551$. One recovered generator instance is not a coverage study.

\subsection{Step 1: independent reference equations and matched inputs}
The reference \code{evaluation/wqeval/reference.py} imports NumPy/SciPy without importing \code{memorylab}. For column vectorization and Hamiltonian $G=\mathrm{diag}(0,\delta)$ in Hz, it uses
\[
\mathcal L=-2\pi i(I\otimes G-G^{\mathsf T}\otimes I)
+\sum_J\left[J^*\otimes J-\tfrac12 I\otimes J^\dagger J
-\tfrac12(J^\dagger J)^{\mathsf T}\otimes I\right].
\]
The jump operators are $\sqrt{\Gamma_1(1-p_e)}\,|0\rangle\langle1|$, $\sqrt{\Gamma_1p_e}\,|1\rangle\langle0|$, and $\sqrt{\Gamma_\phi/2}\,Z$. The stage superoperator is $\exp(t\mathcal L)$. Write and read stages include their specified depolarizing maps, and storage adds the declared network-activity dephasing. The reference constructs $C=\sum_{a,b}|a\rangle\langle b|\otimes\mathcal E(|a\rangle\langle b|)$ by matrix-unit propagation, including an orthogonal erasure outcome for the full channel. It computes six Pauli-eigenstate outputs and their mean target fidelity independently of the lab's Kraus construction.

The four joint parameter choices $(\Gamma_1,\delta,p_e)$ are $(0,0,0)$, $(0.05,123,0.1)$, $(10,-500,0.5)$ and $(100,1000,0.9)$, with rates in s$^{-1}$ and detunings in Hz.
